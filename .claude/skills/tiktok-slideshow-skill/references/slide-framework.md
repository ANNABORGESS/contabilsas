# The SLIDE framework

A TikTok photo post is judged like a video: does the viewer swipe past slide 1, and do they reach
the app? Every rule here serves that. The bar for every slide: it passes for a post by a real
person, not a brand.

## S: Stop (slide 1)

Slide 1 is the thumbnail, the hook and the only slide many people see.

**Hook rules**

- **About the viewer or another person, never about the app.** "habit apps when you miss ONE
  day:" beats "Meet HabitApp". A disagreement with someone else ("my sister said...") is one of
  the strongest openers when it really happened.
- **Specific.** A moment, a number, a named situation: "ONE day", "47 days", "the night before
  finals".
- **Readable in under 2 seconds.** About 4 to 10 words.
- **An open loop.** Something the next slides resolve: what happens, who was right, what fixes it.
- **No app name, no logo, no price** on slide 1.
- **True.** Never promise something the slideshow doesn't deliver.

**Hook patterns** (fill them with the user's real situation):

| Pattern | Shape | Needs a real story? |
|---|---|---|
| Relatable scenario | "[category] apps when you [small failure]:" | No |
| Someone else, conflict | "my [person] said [doubt], so I showed them [result]" | Yes |
| Tension | "[the moment the problem hurts]" | No |
| Honest test | "I tried [N] [category] apps for [time]" | Yes |
| Mistake list | "[N] mistakes I made [doing X]" | Yes |
| Tiny tutorial | "how to [do X] in [short time]" | No |
| Be honest | "be honest: how many [things] have you [abandoned]?" | No |

Write 10 to 20 hooks per session, use the best 3 to 5, log the rest.

## L: Lead in (slide 2)

Make the problem felt: the moment, the frustration, the stakes. "fell asleep once. streak: 0." One
slide, not three.

## I: Introduce (slide 2 or 3)

The app appears early, as the answer to slide 1. Most viewers never reach slide 5.

- Name it in the slide text: "plodd: miss a day, nothing resets".
- Show it for real: a full-screen screenshot (`"layout": "fullscreen"`) or a photo of the phone
  showing the app. Not a floating mockup.
- Say what it does in plain words, not features ("AI-powered engagement engine").

## D: Deliver (1 or 2 slides)

What changes for the viewer: "it just shows how your week actually went". Specific, small,
believable.

## E: End (last slide)

One ask:

- A question that invites comments ("be honest, how many habit apps have you deleted?").
- "Link in bio" only when the profile has the link.
- "Search [name]" only when the agent has checked that [name] actually shows the app first on
  TikTok and Google. Otherwise it sends viewers to a competitor.
- A follow reminder is worth testing on some posts.
- Don't ask for likes.

## Text rules

- **One text block per slide**, one size, 1 to 3 short lines. A headline plus a small line under
  it reads like a presentation.
- Write like people post: mostly lowercase, plain words, contractions, no marketing language, no
  emoji walls.
- Main search words in the caption's first words; on-slide text in words people actually type.

## Design rules

- **One photo set per slideshow:** the same person or room, the same light. Never six strangers.
- **No literal illustrations** (a clock for "strict", a cat for "pet"). Photos set the scene.
- **One text style per slideshow**, set in `theme`: `highlight` (strips, choose white or black
  once) or `classic` (white with outline and soft shadow). TikTok Sans, bundled.
- **Text on calm space, never over a face.** Move it with `position`.
- **The app slide is real:** a full-screen screenshot or the phone in someone's hand.

## Caption and hashtags

- First words: the main search phrase in natural words ("habit tracker without streaks").
- Then one line of context and a question.
- 1 to 3 specific hashtags (#habittracker, #productivityapps). Skip #fyp and #viral.

## The honesty gate

Run this before rendering the final version. Any "no" means rewrite that slide.

1. Every claim is something the user actually did, saw or can prove. Every first-person line
   ("I tried", "my sister said", "my users") comes from the user; the agent never writes their
   experiences.
2. No invented user counts, revenue, downloads, ratings, reviews or testimonials.
3. Comparisons are fair: real apps the user actually tried, no insults, no made-up flaws.
4. Every screenshot is real and current. No mocked-up results.
5. All images are the user's own, licensed (Pexels, Unsplash free license) or generated for this
   post.
6. Realistic AI images will be disclosed on TikTok.
7. Sponsored or client posts will use TikTok's branded-content setting.
8. Health, money and legal topics: no promised outcomes.
