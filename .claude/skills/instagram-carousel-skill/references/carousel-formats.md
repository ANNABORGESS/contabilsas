# Carousel formats

Every format below has a job (saves, sends or trust), a slide-by-slide template and a cover
pattern. Fill the templates with the user's real knowledge; never with invented results.

Slide 1 is the cover. Slide 2 is the second cover: Instagram may show the carousel again
starting at slide 2 to people who scrolled past, so slide 2 must make sense on its own.

## Step-by-step (saves, 6 to 10 slides)

1. Cover: "How to [result] in [N] steps" or "[Result] without [pain]".
2. The result or the proof, shown (a screenshot, a number the user can show), plus "here's the
   exact process".
3. to N+2. One step per slide: "Step k: [verb] [thing]" plus one tip or one mistake to avoid.
4. Recap: the steps as a `list` slide.
5. Last slide: "Save this for the next time you [situation]."

## Listicle (saves or sends, 7 to 12 slides)

1. Cover: "[N] [things] for [specific person]" or "[N] [things] I wish I knew before [moment]"
   (the second only if it's the user's real experience).
2. Item 1, the strongest one, so slide 2 works as a cover.
3. One item per slide, each with a one-line why.
4. Optional bonus item.
5. Last slide: "Send this to someone who [specific situation]."

## Myth vs fact (sends, 6 to 10 slides)

1. Cover: the myth, struck through (`~~myth~~`), or "Stop [common advice]."
2. The one-line truth: "What actually works: [thesis]."
3. One `myth` slide per pair: the myth struck through, the fact under it, a `source` when the
   fact is a number or a study.
4. The rule to remember.
5. Last slide: a question ("Which one did you believe?") or a send ask.

## Framework (saves, 5 to 8 slides)

1. Cover: the framework's name and what it gets you.
2. The whole framework on one slide (a short list or a simple diagram).
3. One part per slide, with an example.
4. The whole framework again as the summary.
5. Last slide: save ask.

## Case study (trust, 8 to 12 slides): real results only

1. Cover: the outcome or the tension, as a number the user can prove.
2. The stake: what was at risk or what almost went wrong.
3. Context, the problem, the turning point: one slide each.
4. Lessons, one per slide.
5. Last slide: follow or send ask.

Ask the user for the proof before writing. No proof, no case study: use step-by-step instead.

## Before/after (trust, 4 to 8 slides): real results only

1. Cover: the after, or a split before/after.
2. The before.
3. One change per slide; a `beforeafter` slide shows both screenshots stacked.
4. The after again, with the result.
5. Last slide: how, or a question.

## Tweet-card thread (sends, 6 to 10 slides)

1. Cover: the opening tweet, written as a hook ("3 things a habit app should *never* do").
2. One point per card, under 280 characters.
3. Last card: the ask.

Use the user's own name, handle and avatar. Never fake likes, reposts or view counts, and never
make a card that looks like someone else posted it.

## Photo story (mood, behind the scenes, 5 to 10 slides)

1. Cover: the strongest photo, with 8 words or fewer on it.
2. The second strongest photo (it may be the first one people see).
3. More photos, text on at most a few of them.
4. Last slide: a question or a send ask, as text or on a photo.

One shoot per carousel: same person or place, same light.

## Cover patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Result without pain | "Missed a day? Your habit is fine." |
| Number + specific person | "5 rules for people who restart every Monday" |
| Stop doing | "Stop tracking streaks." |
| Myth struck through | "~~Never miss a day~~" |
| Question they ask themselves | "Why do I quit every habit by week 2?" |
| Before/after (real) | "Our signup page, before and after" |

Rules: 8 words or fewer, specific, about the reader, no clickbait the slides don't pay off.

## Last-slide asks

- **Send:** "Send this to the friend who [specific situation]." The strongest ask for reaching
  new people: Instagram has said sends matter for reach beyond followers.
- **Save:** "Save this for the next time you [situation]."
- **Comment:** a question with an easy answer ("Which rule do you break most?").
- **Follow:** "Follow for [specific thing] every [day of the week]" only if that's true.
- **Link:** "Link in bio" only if the link is there.

One ask per carousel. A last slide with three asks gets none.

## Honesty check

Any "no" means rewrite that slide before rendering.

1. Every claim is something the user did, saw or can show. Every first-person line ("I tried",
   "our users", "we grew") comes from the user; the agent never writes their experiences.
2. No invented numbers: no user counts, revenue, growth, percentages or study results without a
   source the user can point to.
3. No fake reviews, testimonials, tweets or screenshots.
4. Screenshots are real and current.
5. Photos are the user's own, licensed (Pexels and Unsplash licenses allow commercial use) or
   generated for this post, and realistic AI images are labelled.
6. Health, money and legal topics: no promised outcomes.
